Applied research starts with attention: to the shape of a problem, to the people living with it, and to the evidence that changes our minds.

01 / Approach

Rigorous enough to trust.
Practical enough to use.

  1. Frame

    Frame the real question

    Begin with context. Separate the visible symptom from the deeper question, then define what a useful answer would make possible.

  2. Target

    Make evidence tangible

    Use focused research and small experiments to turn assumptions into things we can inspect, challenge, and improve.

  3. Trace

    Leave a clear trail

    Share the reasoning, limits, and practical implications so the work can travel beyond the place it began.

02 / Feedback

From signal to
response.

Collect data from the world as it is. Use automation—scripts, models, and other tools—to turn repeated signals into insight. Let that insight guide action, whether a person decides, a system responds, or both work together. Then observe the result and begin again: adapting and refining with every pass.

Data

Observe and collect signals from the world as it changes.

Insight

Let scripts, models, and other tools surface patterns and update what the system knows.

Action

Respond automatically, by human decision, or through both—and return the result to observation.

observe · interpret · respond · repeat

A place for work

Good inquiry is a practice of returning: to the question, to the evidence, and to the assumptions beneath both. The cards gather prompts for that return—ways to notice what is missing, test what seems settled, and keep useful questions in motion.
